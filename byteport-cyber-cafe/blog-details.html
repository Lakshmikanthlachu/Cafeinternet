7 habits that keep you safe on a public PC

Log out, clear sessions, avoid saving passwords — a short checklist we tape to every monitor.

Leo OkaforTech support · Mar 12, 2026 · 5 min read
Share

Public computers are brilliant: fast, cheap and there when your laptop isn’t. But a few small habits decide whether you leave with everything done — or leave your inbox open for the next person.

Why public PCs need habits

Every Byteport station wipes itself on logout, but not every café does. Building the same routine everywhere means you’re protected whether you’re here, at a library or an airport kiosk.

Always sign out properly

Closing the browser tab isn’t the same as signing out. Use the account menu to sign out, then close the window. On shared PCs, a private window (Ctrl + Shift + N) means cookies vanish the moment you close it.

“If you wouldn’t leave it on a café table, don’t leave it logged in on a café PC.”

Never save passwords

When the browser offers to remember a password, choose Never. Better still, use your phone’s password manager and type the code manually, or sign in with a one-time QR login.

  • Decline “remember me” checkboxes
  • Turn on two-step verification for email
  • Change your password if you ever forget to sign out

Mind your downloads

Files land in Downloads and may stay there on unmanaged PCs. Print or upload them, then delete them and empty the recycle bin. Our help desk can show you how in under a minute.

Use HTTPS and trusted Wi-Fi

Look for the padlock before entering anything personal, and only join the network name posted at the counter. Ask staff if you’re unsure — it’s literally what we’re here for. See our services for more help.
